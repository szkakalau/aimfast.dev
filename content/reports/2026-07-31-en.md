## 🎯 Today's 2-Hour Build

**Product Name**: `FoodFinder` — A Chrome extension / mobile app that overlays a "meals under $10" layer on Google Maps, helping users quickly find cheap, good food.

- **One-liner**: A user-crowdsourced "cheap eats" map that solves the daily pain point: "I'm hungry, but I don't want to spend more than $10."
- **Supporting Evidence**: Today on HN, `CheapFoodMap` sparked **241 comments** with intense user enthusiasm. Many comments discuss "cheap eats in my area" and "how this feature should work." This is ironclad proof of real demand.
- **Why Not the Other Two**:
    1.  **Claude Code parallel agent tools** (e.g., `claude-code-merge-queue`): Cross-platform validation (2 independent sources), but it's a **developer tool** with buyers being "developers using Claude Code." The market ceiling is low, and competition is fierce (3 similar products launched today).
    2.  **GPT-5.6 price drop**: This is a **platform-level event**, not something you can build. You can only wrap it, but wrapping has zero competitive moat, and OpenAI will capture most of the value.
- **Pricing**: **$2.99 one-time purchase** (Chrome extension) or **free download + $4.99/year to remove ads** (App).
- **Fastest Validation Path**:
    1.  Create a simple survey with Google Forms: "In your city, can you recommend a good restaurant under $10?"
    2.  Reply in the HN `CheapFoodMap` discussion thread with the survey link, saying, "I'm planning to build a better version and want to hear your thoughts."
    3.  If you collect 50+ valid data points within 24 hours, demand is viable. Then spend a weekend building a minimal Chrome extension that manually pins these points on Google Maps.
- **Keep MVP Manual**: The first version doesn't need a backend or crawler. Manually scrape posts from Reddit's `r/CheapEats`, `r/EatCheapAndHealthy`, etc., and manually pin them on the map.
