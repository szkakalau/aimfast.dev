## 📝 Editor's Note

On the surface, the community is talking about "terminal speed" and "open source faith" — Shitty terminal pulled 150 comments, and Devtools must be open source sparked 189 discussions. But the truly buildable signals are hiding in two places: first, the collision between **"LLMs reward expertise" (549 upvotes/241 comments)** and "AI has degraded my 5 abilities" (w2solo) — the market is paying for "how to maintain professional judgment in the AI era"; second, consumer tools like **Picture Enhancer (image deblurring)** and **audio/video-to-text converters** are quietly validating demand on w2solo/V2EX. Who pays first? **Senior developers drowning in AI-generated code who fear their skills are atrophying**, plus **everyday users who need to restore blurry old photos or transcribe meeting recordings**. Why this week? Because "AI skill degradation" went from personal musings to a Stack Overflow hot post (188 answers) — the anxiety has hit a tipping point. Is a $19 "AI Code Review Checklist" worth it? Yes, because what it saves you is the weekend you'd otherwise lose debugging hallucinated AI code that made it into production. The real hard part: turning the vague concept of "professional judgment" into actionable checklist items.

## 🎯 Today's 2-Hour Build

### Product: AI Code Review Checklist

**One-liner**: A printable checklist that can also be imported as Cursor rules — helps developers spot "hallucination patterns" in AI-generated code. Not a vague "review carefully," but 20 specific, checkable pattern-recognition rules.

**Supporting Evidence**:
- Stack Overflow hot post "How can I avoid using LLMs as a software developer?" — 188 answers/130 votes
- w2solo article "I wrote code with AI for half a year — looking back, these 5 abilities are degrading" — 34 points
- HN hot post "LLMs reward expertise" — 549 upvotes/241 comments
- HN hot post "Prevent cognitive debt by manually retyping LLM-generated code" — 406 upvotes/343 comments

**Why not the other two**:
- ❌ **Shitty terminal**: 150 comments/2 platforms verified, but buyer_clarity=1 (nobody knows who'd pay for a terminal), and "memory-unsafe" is a turn-off, not a selling point
- ❌ **Nightcrawler mobile AI penetration testing**: High technical barrier, buyers need security/compliance backgrounds, too heavy for a solo dev, and significant legal risk

**Pricing**: $19 one-time (PDF + Markdown), $9/month subscription (continuously updated rule library + auto-synced Cursor rule files)

**Fastest Validation Path**: Doable today — write 20 review rules, package as a Notion template + PDF, post to the HN "LLMs reward expertise" thread and the w2solo "skill degradation" article comments. 50 email signups = validation.

**Keep MVP Manual**: Use Google Forms to collect pain points + manually organize rules. Don't build a platform from day one.

**Counter-view**: If AI code review gets built into Cursor/Windsurf (they're working on it), this product dies within 3 months. So the rule library must be tied to "the human" not "the tool" — you're selling judgment training, not automation.
